## Marketplace model — coach reasoning, not company internal definitions

Consumer discovers an available meal → starts/places order → merchant handles preparation and driver matching occurs → pickup → delivery → support/refund/reorder if needed. Exact matching and preparation timing can vary; clarify in each case.
Liquidity is local and time-dependent: enough suitable meals and fulfillment capacity for demand at a promised price/time. A citywide average can conceal a lunch-hour bottleneck.
Consumer outcomes: total price, selection, reliable arrival and repeat use.
Merchant outcomes: incremental demand, preparation load, waste/cancellation, sustainable earnings.
Driver outcomes: productive time, pickup wait, distance, acceptance, earnings after work-related costs.
Platform outcomes: completed orders and durable retention subject to service and economic constraints.

Illustrative metric identities require consistent population/time definitions:
completed orders = placed orders × completion rate;
placed orders = eligible sessions × session-to-order conversion (only with a compatible attribution/counting definition).
Contribution reasoning = attributable platform revenue minus included variable costs; request accounting definitions, incentive payer, refunds and delivery costs. GMV is not revenue or profit.
ETA should be decomposed into critical-path stages; parallel preparation and matching cannot always be added naively.
Experiments can affect shared driver/merchant capacity, so user-level A/B groups may interfere. Consider geographic/time designs when relevant; do not assume every experiment needs one.
